edit

The history of the changes to a talk or a walk, the Undo of a walk path, and the rewrite of a field with an instruction.

A talk and a walk path have one editor style. Each change saves at once. Then an Undo bar shows, and a history keeps the change, so you can go back. This module is the part of the editor that has no web page:

  • the history of one talk or one walk, kept in the vault, so it is there after a restart;
  • the Undo of a walk path, which keeps your progress on each step;
  • the rewrite of the text of a slide or a step, from a short instruction such as “simpler”;
  • the changes to a walk path: move, skip, remove, merge, add and split a step, and pick a real passage.

The web parts (the Undo bar, the history list, the list of rows and its keys) are in abhyasa/core/edit.py.

Import the shelf, the JSON call of the model, the prompts and the fence for source text.

The limits

A history keeps at most HISTORY_MOST changes, and at most HISTORY_BYTES of JSON. Older changes drop off first. The newest change always stays.

When you type in a field, the editor saves each time you stop for 600 ms. These saves must not fill the history. A save names its field: the row and the field, such as s5/notes. A save of the same field by the same writer, less than EDIT_JOIN seconds after the first save of the newest change, joins that change. Its Undo goes back to the text before the first save. A join does not reset the clock: one long typing session gives one change for each minute, so an Undo never takes back more than about one minute of typing. A change with no field never joins.

When the newest change alone is over HISTORY_BYTES, the history keeps a short form of it: its id, time, words and writer, with before set to None. Undo cannot give back a change in its short form.

The history of one thing

The history of one talk or one walk is one vault document, with the source learning://edits/{kind}/{doc} and the kind edits. For example, the kind is talk and the doc is the id of the talk. Each change has an id, the time at, the words what, the writer who, and before: the whole list of slides or steps before the change. Search never shows these documents.

edit_lock

def edit_lock(
    kind:str, doc:str
)-><function RLock at 0x7fabcf3a3ce0>:

The lock of the history of doc. add_edit and pop_edit hold it while they change the thing and its history, so a save and an Undo from two tabs are one step each. Take it before the lock of a walk (journey._jlock), never after it. It is not the Learning._lock of the concepts: a walk save can hold its own lock and then put a concept, so the two locks in the other order could wait on each other.

add_edit keeps one change, newest first. who is you or tutor. before must be JSON. Bad values raise ValueError. With apply, it calls apply() (the save of the change) under edit_lock first, and keeps the change only when the save works. So a save and its change in the history are one step.

The Undo of a walk path

A walk step has fields that the editor changes (EDIT_FIELDS: the status, the why, the note, the check, its code, the passage and the merged ideas), and fields that the walk writes as you learn: the progress (done, qid, read_at), the side walks, the notebook, the Leela stamp and the research state. The tutor note is content: you edit it, so an Undo gives back the old note. The walk can write its own fields after you changed the path. An Undo must not lose them.

restore_steps gives the order of before. Each step that is also in now (by its slug) is the step of now, with the editor fields of before. It also takes the read of now when research wrote it (researched=True) and the step in before had no passage. A step that is only in before comes back as it was. A step that is only in now goes: the change being undone added it.

The code of a step belongs to its check (qid). When the walk made the check again after the change, the old code does not fit the new check, so the step keeps the code of now. undo_line then gives the line CODE_GONE, for the editor to show.

restore_steps

def restore_steps(
    before:list, now:list
)->list:

The steps of before in their order. A slug that is also in now is the step of now with the EDIT_FIELDS of before, but a passage that research wrote after before, and the code of a check made again, stay.

Step a moves back to the first place. It keeps the passage that research found, and b keeps its outcome.

before = [dict(slug='a', read=dict(text='')), dict(slug='b', read=dict(text='p'))]
now = [dict(slug='b', read=dict(text='p'), done=dict(outcome='solid')), dict(slug='a', read=dict(text='w', researched=True))]
out = restore_steps(before, now)
test_eq([s['slug'] for s in out], ['a', 'b']); test_eq(out[0]['read']['text'], 'w'); test_eq(out[1]['done'], dict(outcome='solid'))

A step that the change added goes. A step that the change removed comes back. before does not change.

out = restore_steps([dict(slug='a'), dict(slug='c', qid='q1')], [dict(slug='a', qid='q2'), dict(slug='new')])
test_eq(out, [dict(slug='a', qid='q2'), dict(slug='c', qid='q1')])
test_eq(restore_steps(before, now)[0]['read'], dict(text='w', researched=True)); test_eq(before[0]['read'], dict(text=''))

An Undo of a note edit gives back the old note: the note is content, not a run field.

test_eq(restore_steps([dict(slug='a', note='old')], [dict(slug='a', note='new')]), [dict(slug='a', note='old')])

A side walk, a notebook, a Leela stamp and the research state that the walk wrote after the snapshot stay. The editor fields come back.

b = [dict(slug='a', status='skip', research='pending')]
n = [dict(slug='a', status='todo', detours=[dict(slug='x')], nb=dict(cells=[]), leela_at=7., research='none')]
test_eq(restore_steps(b, n), [dict(slug='a', status='skip', detours=[dict(slug='x')], nb=dict(cells=[]), leela_at=7., research='none')])

A step whose check the walk made again keeps the new code and the new check, and undo_line says so. With the same check, the old code comes back.

b, n = [dict(slug='a', qid='q1', code=dict(stub='old'))], [dict(slug='a', qid='q2', code=dict(stub='new'))]
test_eq(restore_steps(b, n), [dict(slug='a', qid='q2', code=dict(stub='new'))]); test_eq(undo_line(b, n), CODE_GONE)
n = [dict(slug='a', qid='q1', code=dict(stub='edited'))]
test_eq(restore_steps(b, n)[0]['code'], dict(stub='old')); test_eq(undo_line(b, n), '')

Rewrite with an instruction

You tell the tutor what to change, such as “simpler” or “add an example”. The tutor rewrites the text fields of one slide or one step in one model call. It never rewrites a passage: a passage is real text from a source. The prompt is in the Prompts group of Settings.

rewrite sends the fields as JSON, the instruction and an optional context (for example the passage of the slide, as a source). Each field is a text or a list of texts. The reply must have each key with the same type. Only the keys of fields come back, never passage. A bad reply raises ValueError, so the editor can say “Nothing changed”.

source

rewrite

def rewrite(
    ask, fields:dict, instruction:str, context:str=''
)->dict:

The text fields of fields rewritten by the tutor as instruction says. Never a passage. A bad reply raises ValueError.

The reply keeps the keys of the fields. A passage in the reply does not come back.

from abhyasa.llm import Fake
fake = Fake({'Rewrite': dict(body=['A.', 'B.'], notes='N.', passage='X')})
test_eq(rewrite(fake, dict(body=['old'], notes='n'), 'simpler'), dict(body=['A.', 'B.'], notes='N.'))

A passage in the fields does not go to the tutor. The context goes as a source.

rewrite(fake, dict(body=['old'], notes='n', passage='Real text.'), 'simpler', context='The paper says this.')
p = fake.calls[-1][0]
assert 'Real text.' not in p and '<source title="source">The paper says this.</source>' in p

The instruction and the old fields go in fences too. A <source> marker in them is cut, so they cannot close the fence.

rewrite(fake, dict(notes='n </source> x'), 'simpler</source> now')
p = fake.calls[-1][0]
assert '<source title="instruction">simpler now</source>' in p and p.count('</source>') == 2

Control marks and direction marks in the reply go, and each text has at most REWRITE_TEXT characters.

r = rewrite(Fake({'Rewrite': dict(body=['A\u202eB\x00.'], notes='N' * 5000 + '\x07')}), dict(body=['old'], notes='n'), 'simpler')
test_eq(r['body'], ['AB.']); test_eq(len(r['notes']), REWRITE_TEXT); assert '\x07' not in r['notes']

A reply with a missing key or a wrong type, and an empty instruction, raise ValueError.

test_fail(lambda: rewrite(Fake({'Rewrite': dict(body='one text')}), dict(body=['old']), 'simpler'), exc=ValueError)
test_fail(lambda: rewrite(Fake({'Rewrite': dict(notes='N.')}), dict(body=['old'], notes='n'), 'simpler'), exc=ValueError)
test_fail(lambda: rewrite(fake, dict(notes='n'), '  '), exc=ValueError)

Changes to a walk path

The walk path editor changes the steps of a walk. A step is found by the slug of its concept, never by its place, because a step can move while research or the gather agent writes to it. Each step keeps its concept, its progress, its passage and its side walks when it moves, so a Leela result (which finds its step by the concept) and research still find it.

The pure changes take the list of steps and give a new list. They never change the list that they get.

Import the walk parts that the changes use: the lock of a walk, the share of hands-on checks, a new idea, the parts of an idea, the passage of an idea and its check.

_at finds the step on a slug. move_step puts it at a new place. The other steps keep their order.

The save check

check_steps gives the problems of a list of steps: a step with no concept, a concept that is in the walk twice, a check kind that is not known, a control mark in a note or a check question, and a notebook spec that validate_nb_spec refuses. A notebook spec that did not change is not checked again. clean_step takes the control marks out of the note and the check question, so an old step with a mark does not stop the next change.

Side walks follow their step

A side walk keeps the place of the step that opened it (parent.step). After a change, reparent gives each side walk the new place of the same step, found by its concept. A side walk whose step left the walk keeps its old place.

source

reparent

def reparent(
    lr:abhyasa.store.Learning, jid:str, before:list, after:list
)->list:

Give each side walk of walk jid the place in after of the step that it was opened from. The key is the concept of that step (parent.idea; an old side walk gets it from its place in before), and it never changes. A step that a merge joined maps to the step that it joined. A removed step gives no place (step None) until an Undo brings the step back. Return the side walk ids that changed.

One change, kept with its Undo

edit_walk makes one change to walk jid and keeps it in the history, as one step. It takes edit_lock first and then the lock of the walk (_jlock), so a save from research or the gather agent waits. It reads the steps, applies op, and checks the result with check_steps. A change that changes nothing keeps nothing. what says what changed; it can be a function of the old and the new steps. A problem raises ValueError, and nothing is saved.

source

edit_walk

def edit_walk(
    lr:abhyasa.store.Learning, jid:str, op, what, who:str='you', field:str=None
)->tuple:

Apply op (old steps -> new steps) to walk jid and keep the change in its history. Return (walk, history); the history is None when nothing changed.

undo_walk takes back the newest change (only when eid is still the newest). It writes back the steps of before with restore_steps, so the progress and a researched passage stay, and the side walks follow. It returns the change and the line of undo_line. restore_walk goes back to the version before one change, and keeps that as a new change, so you can undo it.

test_eq(lr.edits('walk', 'w1'), [])

The key is the concept, so a remove and its Undo put the side walk back on the same step, not on the step that took its place. A merge maps it to the step that it joined.

lr.put_journey(dict(id='w3', title='W', mode='walk', steps=[dict(slug='a'), dict(slug='b'), dict(slug='c')]))
lr.put_journey(dict(id='side3', title='S', mode='walk', steps=[], parent=dict(walk='w3', step=1)))
s3 = lr.journey('w3').steps
edit_walk(lr, 'w3', lambda ss: remove_step(ss, 'b'), 'Removed “b”.')
test_eq(lr.journey('side3').parent, dict(walk='w3', step=None, idea='b'))
undo_walk(lr, 'w3'); test_eq(lr.journey('side3').parent, dict(walk='w3', step=1, idea='b'))
edit_walk(lr, 'w3', lambda ss: merge_steps(ss, 'a'), 'Merged “a” and “b”.'); test_eq(lr.journey('side3').parent['step'], 0)
undo_walk(lr, 'w3'); test_eq(lr.journey('side3').parent, dict(walk='w3', step=1, idea='b')); test_eq(lr.journey('w3').steps, s3)

A change that changes nothing keeps nothing. A change with a problem raises ValueError and saves nothing.

test_eq(edit_walk(lr, 'w1', lambda ss: ss, 'Nothing.')[1], None)
test_fail(lambda: edit_walk(lr, 'w1', lambda ss: ss + [dict(slug='a')], 'Added a.'), contains='twice')
test_eq(lr.journey('w1').steps, b); test_eq(lr.edits('walk', 'w1'), [])

A restore is a change too, so an Undo takes it back.

edit_walk(lr, 'w1', lambda ss: remove_step(ss, 'a'), 'Removed “a”.')
restore_walk(lr, 'w1', lr.edits('walk', 'w1')[0].id)
test_eq([s['slug'] for s in lr.journey('w1').steps], ['a', 'b']); test_eq(len(lr.edits('walk', 'w1')), 2)

Add and split

add_step adds an idea at place at: by its name (the tutor writes a new concept when the shelf has none), by the slug of a concept from the concept tree, or with a read that you picked from a source. The new step gets a check. With no read, it looks for a passage in the documents of the walk and in sources, as a new walk does. A step with no passage then gets “Find a source” on its page. The model call runs before the locks.

split_step asks the tutor for the smaller parts of an idea (split_parts), then adds each as a step before it, with a passage and a check.

Real passages to pick

passages gives the real passages that a step can take: the paragraphs of the documents of the walk (its source, the pages of a link, and the document of each passage), word for word. A walk with no document gives the passages that ground finds in sources for the name of the idea. q keeps the passages that have each word of q. The passages that name the idea come first. pick_read gives the read of a text that you picked, only when it is in one of these passages word for word (verbatim). The tutor never writes a passage.

Improve the walk

The learner can tell the tutor what to change in a walk, in their own words: make it simpler or harder, or add an idea, with a link as its source. propose asks the tutor for a short list of changes. Each change uses one op of the walk editor: add (with a name, and a link when the learner gave one), remove, skip, move (with to, the new place from 1), split, easier or harder (the depth of the check goes down or up by one). Each change has a one-line why. A change that names no idea of the walk, an op that the editor does not have, or a link that the learner did not give is left out. links_in finds the links in the words of the learner.

improve_walk applies the changes that the learner kept, as one change with one Undo. It writes the new ideas before it takes the lock: an idea from a link gets its passage from the documents of that link (docs maps a link to them), word for word. The ideas that the changes do not touch keep their progress.

guide

The screen guide: “I don’t get X”, answered with a board that the tutor draws over the page.

The learner looks at a walk step, a concept or a video, and says “I don’t get alpha bar”. The guide answers on the page, as a teacher does at a whiteboard. It circles the term, draws an arrow to a label in the figure, moves the slider of the demo, and speaks one short line for each step.

This module is the part of the guide that has no web page:

  • the board language: a closed list of drawing commands, each tied to a thing on the page (an anchor), and the check of each line of the model’s reply;
  • the context: what the page shows, cleaned and cut to size;
  • the gate: the tutor answers at once when it is sure, else it looks in your vault, else on the web;
  • the guard: no secret and no personal data leaves the Mac in a model call or a web query.

The guide block serves it, and core/board.js draws it.

Import the helpers. The model layer and ramabana load only on the first call, so the import stays light.

The board language

A reply is JSON lines: one object on each line. The host checks each line alone, so a bad line drops and the rest draws. There are 14 commands. The model never writes HTML, CSS, script or a URL.

op fields what it does
step say, and src with quote starts a step; say is the spoken line. A quote is word for word from source src
circle, box, underline at a hand-drawn ring, box or line on the anchor
arrow from, to a curved arrow between two anchors
label at, text, side a short note beside the anchor
sketch at, pts a free line in the box of a figure anchor, to mark a part of the figure
point at moves a pointer mark to the anchor
clear n (optional) removes the marks of all steps, or of step n
demo_set set changes inputs of the step’s rupaka demo, only after its gate is open
video_at t, quote moves the explainer video to t seconds, and quotes its transcript there
panel title, head, cols, numbered (each optional) starts the side panel at the top right of the window: a small title, a bold heading and the names of two columns. A new panel starts a new beat
row cells, and to, at, chain (optional) adds one row to the side panel: the cells as boxes, then an arrow and to. With at, the page circles that thing and a hand-drawn curve links it to the row. With chain, the cells are words with arrows between them
note text one quiet line in the side panel

Each stroke can have w: the word of say where it starts.

A sketch marks a part of a figure only, so no shape covers the words of the page. A picture of an idea goes in the side panel. For a formula, the tutor gives an example from day-to-day life and marks the page’s own formula.

The side panel is the place for a small concrete example (Explain). The tutor builds it one row in each step, from the real values on the page: its row names the anchor of the value in at. The page circles the value and draws a curve from it to the row, so the model writes no circle for it. A process of 3 to 5 steps is a panel with numbered rows, or one chain row. A new panel line starts the next beat, for example from the values on the page to the big idea. The side panel is the one picture of Explain. Show is a rupaka demo.

The first line can be a header: {"op":"v","n":1,"sure":0.9,"need":"none"}. sure is how sure the model is, from 0 to 1. need says what it needs to be sure: none, vault or web.

Anchors

A stroke names a thing on the page. The page sends a list of anchors, each with an id, a kind and its text. A formula also has its tokens (the TeX of its parts), a figure has its labels (a box for each label in the image), and the demo has its inputs.

form example the place
an id "passage" the whole anchor
words {"a": "passage", "text": "closed form"} the words in the anchor
a maths token {"a": "m1", "tex": "\\bar\\alpha_t"} the part of the formula
a figure label {"a": "figure", "label": "a"} the label in the image
a box {"a": "figure", "box": [100, 200, 400, 600]} a part of the anchor, from 0 to 1000
a point {"a": "m1", "pt": [1100, 500]} a point beside the anchor, for the far end of an arrow

Two ways to write the same TeX must match. norm_tex takes out the spaces and the braces around one symbol.

The check of one line

check_line gives the reason that a line drops, or ''. Each string in a line is checked too: no tag, no script and no link can get in, also as the text of a label.

A cue of the explainer video is [t, text]. cue_at gives the cue that plays at t seconds. cue_quote finds a quote in the cue at t or in the cue before or after it. It gives the start of the cue where the quote begins, and the quote as the transcript says it. The board seeks the video to that start, so a time that the model gives a little late still lands on the right cue.

The whole reply

parse_board reads a reply line by line. Each step keeps at most 6 strokes, and a board has at most 6 steps. A stroke before the first step drops. Two dropped lines in a row end the board (failed): the steps before them stay.

A step can quote a source: src is the number of the source in ctx.sources, and quote must be word for word in its text, with 40 to 600 characters (research.verbatim, the gate that research uses). A quote that fails drops, and the step stays, in the tutor’s words.

The context

The page sends what it shows: its address, the walk step, the concept, the anchors, the demo, the video, and what the learner showed with the pointer (the focus), and the thread: the last questions on this page. clean_context keeps only the keys that it knows and cuts the text: at most 40 anchors, 300 characters an anchor and 6000 in all. An anchor id has only letters, digits and :_-. An unknown kind becomes text. A try anchor is the last graded try of the open check (see The learner’s answer): it keeps its own fields, each with its own bound (TRY). The result is a PageContext. explain cleans each context that is not one, so a raw dict from a host is cleaned too, even when it has a by key.

What the learner shows

While the learner talks or types the question, they can circle a part with the mouse, or hold the pointer still on it. The host finds the gestures and sends them as focus: a list of items, the latest first. Each item has a kind (circle or point), the anchors under it (the best one first), a box from 0 to 1000 of its first anchor, and t, the milliseconds from the start of the question. An item can have words: what the learner said or typed near that time.

_focus keeps at most 5 good items. It keeps only the anchor ids that the page sent, and drops an item with none of them, with a bad kind or with a bad box.

The thread of a page

A learner who asks “say that more simply” or “why?” after a board means that board. So the page keeps a short thread in memory, and sends it with the next question: the last 3 turns on this page, each with the question (said), the lines that its board said (says) and its tier (t). The page forgets the thread when the page changes, after 10 minutes with no question, or when the learner pushes Start fresh. The server keeps no thread: each question brings its own.

_thread keeps the good turns, the oldest first: a question of at most 300 characters, at most 6 lines of at most 160 characters, and a known tier. A turn with no question or no line drops, and so does a turn with a tag or a link in a line, which a board never says. At most 1500 characters stay in all: the oldest turn goes first.

The demo part keeps the inputs (with their value, range and label), the readouts, and whether the gate is open.

The guard

Text that leaves the Mac passes ramabana’s guard (ramabana.guard) first. guard(s, model) is for a model call: the page context and the question, and the prompt of a vision call. It is outgoing for the model of the route. It always masks API keys, tokens and private keys as [SECRET]. For personal data it follows ramabana’s setting cli.pii, but only for a hosted model: redact masks it, refuse withholds the text (Withheld), and off lets it through. A model on this Mac gets the personal data. When the route is not known, guard takes a hosted model (HOSTED).

safe_query(q) is for a web query. It is ramabana’s query: a query with a secret or with personal data does not go, with any setting.

The guard reads text only. A figure image goes to the vision model as it is: no text guard can read pixels.

A board as a hint

On a page with an open check (the anchor check), a board counts as one hint on the check only when it is about the check (on_check): the learner showed the check with the pointer, or named it (“check”, “question”, “answer”, “hint”, “quiz”), or the main words of the question are words of the check (two of them, or all when there are fewer). The guide decides this before it asks, so it can tell the learner first. A board that does not count may not touch the check: the request says so (OFF_CHECK), and a stroke on the check drops. Then the learner keeps the hints for a real hint.

When the page sends the learner’s last try (the anchor try), a question about the try counts too: one that names the gaps, the try, a mistake, an error, what is wrong or the feedback (TRY_ASK), or a pointer on the try. Then the request says so (ON_TRY), and the model follows the rule for a question about an answer. A board that does not count gets no try.

The three tiers

The guide asks in up to three tiers, and the board shows which one it used:

  1. tutor: the page alone. When the model says that it is sure (sure of SURE or more), the board draws at once. The passage on the page is source 1, so a quote from it shows as your source.
  2. vault: else the guide searches your sources (ground) and asks again with the passages that it finds.
  3. web: else, when research is on, it searches the web, reads the pages, and takes a passage that is word for word in the page (research.pick_passage, the gate of research). Then it asks one last time with that passage. The page goes into your vault, linked to the concept. The page says so at the end of the board, with Undo (unsave).

When no tier is sure, the best board so far shows, in the tutor’s own words.

Never caught unaware

The learner must always know what the guide does now, and where the text goes. So before each step of its work, explain sends a state event to on_state. The guide route sends each one to the page as it comes, and the page shows it in one line, the guide bar. The Mac overlay reads the same events. This is the one shape:

{"wait": "<one STE sentence>", "state": "reading" | "asking" | "searching" | "saving" | "drawing",
 "model": "<model label>", "where": "on this Mac" | "hosted", "who": "<company of a hosted model, or ''>",
 "query": "<the web query word for word, only for searching>", "sources": <count of vault passages or web pages that go to the model>}

wait is always a full sentence, because an old page reads only wait. A field that does not apply is not in the event. explain sends asking, searching, reading and saving. The page itself shows reading for its own read, and drawing when the first step draws, so the route never sends a line that covers the board.

When the guide files a web page in the vault, explain sends {"saved": {...}} with the document and the concept link. The route gives the page {"saved": {"title", "url", "undo"}}, where undo is the POST route that takes the page and its link away again (unsave).

where_of (from abhyasa.llm) tells who runs a model. It uses the label of model_label and the locality of ramabana (is_local, which knows the endpoint of an Ollama model). The model rows in Settings use the same function.

The board as it comes

A board takes some seconds to write. So the tutor tier streams it, and the first step draws while the model writes the next one. stream_board reads the reply as it comes. When a new line is in, it reads the whole lines so far again with parse_board, so the rules are the same as for a whole reply. When the header says sure of SURE or more, on_step(step, k) gets each step as soon as its spoken line and its first mark are in, and again each time it gets more strokes: so the first mark does not wait for the rest of the step (the rows of a side panel) or for the next step. The page adds the strokes that it does not have yet. A step that is not sure, or a reply with no header, sends nothing, and the guide goes on to the vault and the web as before. The board that stream_board returns is the truth: its first steps are the steps that went to on_step.

When the stream breaks after a step went out, the board keeps the whole lines that came, so the page does not lose what it shows.

The learner can stop: a newer question, Esc, or a closed tab. Then the guide route sets the threading.Event stop. stream_board reads no more chunks and closes the stream. So the model call ends at once, and the next question does not wait for the backend.

    chunks, ctx, on_step:NoneType=None, sure:float=0.7, stop:NoneType=None

Chunked is a test model that streams its reply in chunks of 7 characters. It writes each chunk in events, and the test writes each step there too, so the order of the events shows when each step went out.

class Chunked(Fake):
    "A `Fake` that can stream: `stream(prompt, sp)` gives its reply in chunks of 7 characters, and notes each one in `events`."
    def __init__(self, *a, **kw): super().__init__(*a, **kw); self.events = []
    def stream(self, p, sp=''):
        out = self(p, sp)
        for i in range(0, len(out), 7): self.events.append('chunk'); yield out[i:i + 7]

The board in the page

core/board.js draws the checked board. It takes its numbers from Clicky (github.com/farzaa/clicky, MIT):

  • The pointer. A small triangle in the board ink flies to each mark on a quadratic arc. The arc lifts by min(d * 0.2, 80) px, the flight takes clamp(d / 800, 0.6, 1.4) s, and the easing is smoothstep. The pointer turns to its path and grows to 1.3x as it lands, then settles on a spring (response 0.4, damping 0.6). A mark draws from its tip.
  • The glow. A box is a soft ring and halo that blooms and then settles. It stays 10 px outside the text, so it never cuts it.
  • The marker. A circle on a formula part or on words adds a wide marker stroke through the glyphs. Its blend mode comes from the surface under it: multiply on a light surface, screen on a dark one. A white figure in dark mode still uses multiply.
  • The chips. A label is a pastel chip with a coloured bar at its left, dark text in a sans font, and a thin leader line that ends in a dot on the part. Each label in a step has its own pastel. The chip pops in on a spring, and it never covers its target.
  • The strokes. Rings, underlines and sketches are perfect-freehand strokes with pressure, and they draw on as a pen does. An arrow is one light Rough.js stroke with a filled head. Marks from earlier steps fade to 45 %, and the glow of an earlier step goes.
  • The side panel. The example of Explain is a card on warm paper at the top right of the window, in a hand font, with the values in monospace. Each step adds its row: the circle on the value on the page at its word, the curve from it 120 ms later, the row when the curve lands, then each cell 80 ms after the one before. Rows stay for the next steps; their curves fade. A new beat (a new panel line) has its own title. While the panel shows, the page column moves left, so the panel never covers a mark. Below 1100 px the panel stands in the page, under the marked part.
  • The scroll. Before a step draws, the page scrolls its first mark into view when the mark is off screen or under the strip.
  • The voice. Each word of the spoken line in the strip lights up when it is said.
  • The state line. While the guide works, the strip shows one line: the wait text of the latest state event, a chip that says on this Mac or hosted · , the seconds after 3 s while the model writes, and Stop. Stop and Esc end the request, so the model stream closes. The caption of a board names its model, and has Voice off and Stop. The end shows what the guide saved, with Undo, and the hint line when the board counted as a hint on the open check.
  • The figure. The picture of a figure goes to the vision model only when the question is about it (the learner showed it, or the question names it), and the line says so first: “The guide sends this figure to to find its labels.”

With prefers-reduced-motion, the pointer jumps to its mark, and the marks and chips fade in for 150 ms. Text from the model goes in with textContent only.

Host hooks: the board on another page

Leela and other hosts use the same board. play takes the hooks of the page in host. Each hook is optional, and the defaults keep the behaviour of abhyasa:

abhyasaBoard.play(board, {labels, fast, quiet, auto, host: {resolve, speak, demo, assets}})
Option What it does Default
labels {anchorId: {label: [x0, y0, x1, y1]}}, the label boxes of a figure, 0 to 1000 of the anchor {}
fast Draw each step at once, with no voice and no flight false
quiet No voice: the words are timed by their letters, and host.speak is not called false
auto Go to the next step 1.2 s after the line is said true (false when fast)
host.resolve(id) -> Element\|null The element of anchor id. A formula element names its parts in data-parts: JSON [[tex, elementId], ...]. The board finds each part by its id in the element, then in the document. A token with no part element marks the whole formula document.querySelector('[data-guide-id="<id>"]')
host.speak(text, onWord, done) Say text. Call onWord(k) when word k (from 0, split on white space) starts, and done() at the end. It can return a function that stops the speech. The board ignores calls after the step changes speechSynthesis, timed by letters when there is no voice
host.demo(set) Move the demo to set, as {input: value}. A host demo must do its own gate check Rupaka.set on [data-guide-id="demo"], only when each gate is done, so the result never shows first
host.assets The URL folder of rough.js and perfect-freehand.mjs '/vendor/'

A host that gets the steps one at a time (the step events of the guide route) starts the board with step 1 and streaming: true, gives each next step to add(step), and gives the whole board to finish(board) at the end. The board waits at its last step until the next one comes. finish gives false when the steps that show are not the first steps of the whole board: then the host plays the whole board. A host that does not know these calls plays the whole board, as before.

abhyasaBoard.play({steps: [step1], tier: 'tutor', sources: [], streaming: true}, opts)
abhyasaBoard.add(step2)            // true, or false when no streaming board shows
abhyasaBoard.finish(board)         // true, or false: then play(board)

A host with a rupaka demo gets its anchors from abhyasaBoard.demoAnchors(host), so that each host uses the same ids: demo for the demo, in:<bind> for each block whose bind is a key of spec.state, and out:<n> for each readout, caption, bars, pixels or plot block that shows, with n from 1 in the depth-first order of the spec. An id of more than 40 characters is dropped, never cut. The server never numbers these ids. clean_context only checks their shape.

abhyasaBoard.state gives {i, steps, tier, first, done, streaming}, and abhyasaBoard.pointer gives {tip, to, flying} in window pixels. Formula parts come from core/texparts.js (window.abhyasaTex): texParts, texAtoms, texWrap, and trust, the one KaTeX trust rule. A host vendors that file to mark its formulas the same way.

Show while you talk

While the learner holds Hyper+E or types in the box, they can show the part they mean with the mouse. guide.js keeps the pointer path in memory only while the guide asks: page pixels, at most 30 points a second, the last 15 s and 600 points. The ask takes the path, finds the gestures, and forgets the path.

Gesture Rule
circle The path turns 300 degrees or more (and not more than 540) in 8 s, comes back near its start (20 px, or 35 % of the loop size), and its box is 24 px or more on each side
point The pointer stays inside 12 px for 400 ms or more, after it came from 40 px or more away. A rest at the end of a circle is part of the circle
nothing A small shake of a hand on the mouse, a swipe, a zigzag, an open arc, a small loop

A circle selects the anchors that it covers most. A point selects the smallest anchor that holds it. A gesture on no anchor gets the element with text under it as a new anchor. The ask sends at most 5 items, the latest first, as ctx.focus: {kind, box, anchors, t, words}. box is from 0 to 1000 of the first anchor, t is in ms from the start of the question, and words are the last words in the box near that time. The Mac overlay sends the same shape, with the same rules (tests/data/gesture_paths.json holds the sample paths that both test).

While the learner draws, a faint trail of the last 1.5 s shows in the board ink, and a soft ring shows on each circle or point for 1.5 s. With prefers-reduced-motion, there is no trail, and the ring does not move.
